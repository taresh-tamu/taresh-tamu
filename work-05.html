Qualification with 80% fewer coupons

At EOS of North America I designed LPBF builds around in-situ monitoring and made the strategy portable across platforms. Coupon count fell by about 80%, with every mechanical and microstructural target still met.

Alloy
Superelastic NiTi
Where
EOS of North America, Metals R&D
When
First half of 2026
My role
R&D intern
Programmes
DELTA-QUAL, ACCELERATE and RECAP (America Makes), VITAL (LIFT)
Methods
DOE, in-situ monitoring

Qualifying a metal AM process is mostly the cost of coupons. Print them, test them, print them again when anything changes. Re-qualification after a machine or platform change is the same bill a second time. If in-situ sensor data can carry part of the evidentiary load, most of those coupons stop being necessary.

At EOS of North America, across the DELTA-QUAL and ACCELERATE programmes (America Makes), I designed LPBF builds around in-situ monitoring and made the strategy portable across tool platforms, so the evidence travelled with the process rather than with the machine. Coupon count fell about 80% with every mechanical and microstructural target still met.

Two related pieces came out of the same five months: a closed-loop powder reuse methodology that cut virgin powder consumption and waste by around 75% (RECAP, America Makes), and the start of VITAL (LIFT), a closed-loop LPBF control and property prediction framework built to align qualification strategies across OEM platforms.
